# Dishwasher: The Friendliest Appliance to Fix

## What error codes honestly mean

A dishwasher error code is a *clue about a symptom*, not a diagnosis. Each manufacturer codes things differently (Bosch, Whirlpool, GE, Samsung, LG all differ), but the stocks usually break down like this:

| Code family | Usually means | Often actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Water not filling (e.g., `E01`–`E15`-ish "no water" codes) | Inlet valve didn't open or water didn't arrive | The under-sink shutoff valve is half-closed, kinked hose, or the inlet screen is clogged |
| Water not draining (e.g., `E24` on Bosch, `OE/iE` on LG) | Pump ran, water still standing | Clogged filter, clogged drain hose, or garbage-disposal knockout plug never removed on new installs |
| Door/lock fault (`E15`-style leak codes, `dO/dL` codes) | Door didn't latch or water leaked where it shouldn't | Door gasket gnarled, too much detergent making suds, or dishwasher tilted forward |
| Heating / wash fault (`E09`, `HE`-type) | Water didn't reach temperature | Hard-water scale on the heater, or just a cold-water-heavy cycle — one retry often clears it |
| Sensor/overflow codes | A float switch tripped | Suds from wrong detergent **or wrong amount** triggering the overflow sensor |

**The honest rule:** if the code mentions water coming in, going out, or suds — try the *FREE* steps below before assuming a dead part. If a code survives a reset *and* a clean filter, that's when it gets interesting.

**A quirk worth knowing:** new dishwashers with "Eco"/energy-saver dry modes often *seem* to fail because dishes came out wet. That's not an error — that's 2020s design trading heated drying for a rinse-aid assist. Use rinse aid and prop the door open at cycle end.

## What's worth cleaning monthly — the real list

1. **The filter (the big one).** Twist out the cylinder filter at the bottom of the tub, rinse under hot water with an old toothbrush. Hard water or eggshells crust it up in weeks. A clogged filter causes "not cleaning," "not draining," and a surprising number of error codes. *FREE, 2 minutes.*
2. **The spray arms.** Fish out the little holes with a toothpick if you see buildup. Spin them by hand to confirm they spin freely.
3. **The door gasket.** Wipe with warm soapy water. Gunk here = slow drip in front of the machine = mysterious floor water nobody can trace.
4. **The edge/interior under the door.** Not glamorous; food molecules live there. A damp microfiber, monthly.
5. **The soap dispenser cup.** Detergent paste builds up and the cup stops snapping open. Wipe it.
6. **Skip:** most dishwashers have no "coil." Dishwasher "maintenance kits" sold for this are mostly a scam.

**Hard-water renters:** a tablespoon of citric acid run through an empty hot cycle monthly beats most "dishwasher cleaners" and costs pennies.

## The honest monthly-ish checklist

- [ ] Filter rinsed (monthly, or weekly if you cook a lot)
- [ ] Gasket wiped
- [ ] Rinse-aid topped up
- [ ] No standing water at the bottom when the machine is idle (standing water = clog forming)

## Reset basics on a dishwasher

- **Basic power cycle:** press Start/cancel to drain-cancel any cycle, then [full reset](reset-basics.html): unplug or flip the breaker for 60 seconds.
- **Cancel-and-drain:** most machines drain a stuck cycle if you press the Cancel button and let it run 1–2 minutes.
- **Control lock ("padlock" icon or `CL`):** hold the button with the padlock icon (or *Heated Dry*/*Air Dry*) 3–5 seconds. This is honestly half of all "my buttons are dead" calls.
- **Firmware:** some Wi-Fi dishwashers update apps more than behavior. If a code appears right after an update, do a full reset before blaming the machine.

## When to call a pro — dishwasher edition

**STOP / call immediately if:**
- Water is leaking onto the floor near any electrical outlet, or under adjacent cabinets — cut the breaker to the dishwasher first, then the under-sink valve.
- Burning smell, buzzing from the motor that suddenly stops, or the breaker trips when it runs.

**Call a pro when:**
- The same water-fill or drain code comes back **after** you've cleaned the filter, checked the shutoff valve and hoses, and reset the machine.
- The door latch is physically broken, or the tub or door is coming apart.
- Standing water returns within a cycle after you've cleared it.
- The inlet valve, pump, or heater likely needs replacing — those are pro-or-confident-DIY jobs.

**Good news:** dishwashers are the cheapest appliance to repair (~$100–250 typical), because everything is accessible and they're basically a pump, a heater, and a computer. Don't fear the dishwasher repair call the way you'd fear a fridge one.

## Replace vs. keep — dishwasher math

- Dishwashers last roughly **9–10 years**, and repairs are cheap and predictable.
- **Keep it** unless: the tub or door is cracked/leaking structurally, or repair quotes exceed half of a new mid-range unit (~$450–700).
- They barely use electricity — mostly hot water — so old dishwashers aren't big energy hogs. Efficiency upgrades here matter less than people think.
