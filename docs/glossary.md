# Glossary: The Words Nobody Explains

Plain-English definitions for words that appear in manuals, error codes, and part listings — and in the chapters of this guide. These are *friend translations*, not engineer-grade definitions.

## Water and plumbing terms

**Condensate** — the water an AC or fridge defrost produces. Always has a pipe of its own, always ends up somewhere you don't want it if the pipe clogs.

**Drain pump** — a small pump that pushes water out of dishwashers and washers. Hates lint, socks, and coasters.

**Float switch** — a buoy in a hidden tray that cuts power when water rises where it shouldn't. In HVAC, it does the same for a clogged condensate line, which is why the AC shuts off politely mid-summer.

**Gasket / door seal** — the rubber ring that makes a door air-tight. Front-loader washers and fridges both live or die by them. They're cheap parts; buy by model number.

**Inlet valve** — electric valve that lets supply water in when the board says so. Its most common failure isn't the valve: it's the *screen* in front of the valve getting clogged.

**Shutoff valve** — the little handle under the sink or behind the machine. Half-closed valves cause "slow fill" codes that look like broken valves.

**Sump / pump filter (washer)** — where washing water collects before being pumped out; on many front-loaders, behind a small panel near the floor. The laundry sock graveyard.

**Trap (P-trap)** — under every sink: the U-shaped pipe that always holds a little water to block sewer smells. The dishwasher drain hose usually branches into it.

## Heating, cooling, and airflow terms

**Capacitor** — a small cylinder that gives motors their "kick" to start. Dead capacitor = motor hums but won't start. Capacitors can hold electrical charge even with power off — a pro-only part for good reason.

**Condenser coils** — fridge: black coils that release heat (clean these). HVAC: the outdoor unit's fins (rinse gently).

**Defrost cycle** — the fridge's scheduled self-thaw. If it malfunctions frost accumulates where fans live and "airflow" codes appear. Manual prolonged defrost is the cheap fix.

**Evaporator coil** — the indoor cold thing in AC (freezes when airflow is starved). In the fridge, it's the part that makes the cold, hidden behind the freezer back panel.

**Fits two breakers (240V)** — dryers, ranges, and some HVAC run on double breakers that trip as a pair; if one flips, the dryer "runs but won't heat." Great trick to check first.

**Limit switch / thermal fuse** — one-use safety parts that die to *protect* you from overheat. They trip because of airflow problems. Replacing a thermal fuse without clearing the cause is how people make the same fire hazard twice.

**Moisture sensor bars** — two metal strips inside the dryer drum that "taste" dampness. Fabric-softener film on them makes the dryer think clothes are wet forever.

**Refrigerant** — the working fluid that makes cooling possible. Leaks/low refrigerant are strictly licensed-tech territory; recharge-can kits are mall-medicine.

**SEER** — an AC efficiency rating; higher is cheaper to run. Old units can have half the SEER of new ones — the fair math behind "old AC is expensive."

**Thermostat(s)** — the wall computer that tells the system what temperature you want. Battery replacement fixes more mystery behavior than any firmware dance.

## Machine & control terms

**Control board** — the appliance's brain. Expensive, but also resettable; power cycles genuinely help.

**Error code** — a sensor's complaint in code form, not a verdict on any part. Always a clue about a symptom.

**Flowmeter / flow sensor** — measures water in (dishwashers and some washers use these). Clogs in front of them fake sensor failures.

**Firmware** — the appliance's built-in software; updates change behavior (occasionally annoyingly). See [reset basics](reset-basics.html).

**Knockout plug** — a small plug in garbage disposals between the dishwasher and the drain line that MUST be knocked out on install; its forgotten-but-not-removed state creates the classic "new dishwasher won't drain" mystery.

**OEM** — original equipment manufacturer part; same part that shipped with the machine. "Equivalent" parts are acceptable when honest sellers label them.

**Pressure switch** — counts (actually senses water level) in washers and dishwashers. Clogged hoses/wires leading to it fake its failure constantly.

**Thermal fuses/heaters in dishwashers** — dishwasher "heating" fault codes frequently get fixed with citric acid descaling, not parts. Fine parts, wrong diagnosis level.

## Warranty, service, and money words

**Diagnostic fee** — the fee a tech charges just to look (often rolls into the repair if you approve it). Ask before scheduling.

**Service plan / home warranty** — a subscription-ish thing that dispatches techs for a copay; read what's covered before buying anything dramatic.

**SEER/energy guide labels** — yellow tags with yearly cost estimates when shopping for fridges/HVAC; the math source for [replace vs. keep](replace-vs-keep.html).
