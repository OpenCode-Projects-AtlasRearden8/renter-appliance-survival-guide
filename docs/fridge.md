# Refrigerator: The Coil Chapter

Fridges are the quiet marathon runners of the house — running 24/7, 365. Two small habits (coils, gaskets) make the biggest difference of anything in this guide.

## What error codes honestly mean

Fridge codes vary wildly by brand, but they cluster around *temperature sensors* — and sensors are exactly the parts that complain when the basics are off.

| Code family | Usually means | Often actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Sensor codes (`Er SS`, `Er rS`, `SY CE` — sensor families) | A temperature sensor reads out of range | Literally a dirty/icy environment confusing it — do the coil-and-gasket basics, defrost, and reset before chasing parts |
| Defrost fault (`Er dH`, `dF`, `rd`) | Defrost cycle didn't complete | Frost buildup behind the back panel; a manual defrost (see below) may fix it for months |
| Fan/airflow fault (`Er FF`, `OF F`) | No airflow between freezer and fridge | Ice blocking the fan path, or a door gasket leaking and frosting everything downstream |
| Door alarm / flashing door icon | Door ajar too long | A jar leaning on the flap, or a worn gasket not sealing |
| `PF` (power failure) | Power was interrupted | Just acknowledge/clear it — it's a note, not a fault |

**The honest rules:**
1. **Fridge codes are often about air, ice, and door seals** — not about the compressor you're worried about.
2. **The compressor clicking on and off every few minutes, or running and never quieting** is the classic overwhelmed-compressor symptom — and its most common cause is dust-caked coils or a leaking door gasket, both cheap.

## What's worth cleaning monthly (ok, every 1–3 months for coils)

1. **Condenser coils — the headline act.** These are the black slats either *under* the fridge (behind the toe-kick grille) or on the *back* (older models). Unplug (or at least flip its breaker), pull the fridge out, and brush/vacuum the dust. Coils covered in dust make the compressor labor, run hot, and die early. *FREE, 10 minutes, every 1–3 months (more if you have pets).*
2. **Door gaskets.** Close the door on a dollar bill at a few heights: if it slides out with no resistance on any side, the gasket is tired. Clean gaskets monthly with warm soapy water; keep them from sticking to the frame by wiping the frame too. Torn gaskets are a common ~$30 DIY part (order by model number).
3. **Drain pan and drain hole.** Under-fridge pan: pull, rinse, replace. The drain hole at the back inside the fridge: a blocked one causes puddles under the produce drawers. Flush with warm water down the hole monthly-ish.
4. **Water dispenser nozzle / drip tray** (ice-maker models): wipe; hard water crusts nozzles.
5. **Door shelves and seals around the doors — the wipe-down.** Spilled milk lives under the door bins and smells like science experiment. Quick wipe monthly.

## Reset basics on a fridge

- **Full reset/power cycle:** fridges benefit most from the 60-second off: unplug or flip breaker, wait, plug back in. It clears sensor fouls and stuck defrost logic better than any button.
- **Manual defrost for ice-fault codes:** empty the freezer, unplug, prop the freezer door open with towels for 8–24 hours (yes, this long — the frost behind the back panel needs to truly melt). This is the legendary fridge fix that solves `dF`-family codes for the price of patience.
- **Control lock and temperature setpoints:** every fridge model resets temps differently; find the lights/lock button combo in your manual. Door alarms almost always clear when the door is properly relatched.
- **Wi-Fi fridges:** firmware updates on fridges fix screens more than cooling; a display acting up deserves a full power cycle before a service call.

## When to call a pro — fridge edition

**STOP / call immediately if:** you ever hear/see/smell anything electrical arcing or burning behind the fridge, or water is pooling where it meets an outlet. Breaker first, TECH call second.

**Call a pro when:**
- The fridge runs but **neither** compartment cools after you've cleaned coils, checked gaskets, and power-cycled — that's compressor/refrigerant territory, which needs a licensed tech (refrigerant handling is legally pro territory).
- Frost returns within weeks after a manual defrost (defrost system part likely failed — a real but common repair).
- Water on the floor whose source you can't trace after checking the drain pan and the water-line connection (ice-maker lines leak under the fridge where you can't see).
- Loud grinding or clicking from the compressor itself.

**Renter/landlord note:** in a rental, the big-ticket fridge failures are landlord items — report promptly and in writing, and keep the coils you cleaned as proof of care.

## Replace vs. keep — fridge math

Fridges are the **#1 appliance where old age actually costs real energy money** — they run constantly.

- Lifespan: **~10–15 years**; after ~12, gaskets, defrost parts, and compressors start failing.
- Pre-2010 fridges can cost **$50–150+/year more** to run than a modern efficient one. That math frequently justifies replacement independent of repairs.
- **Replace** if: it's over ~12 years old and needs any repair beyond a gasket or thermostat, or it's a pre-2005 unit still humming away in the garage (those "extra" garage fridges are silent budget-drains — unplug it and befriend your cooler).
- **Keep** if: it's under ~8 years old, cools well, and just has gasket/coil/ice complaints — all cheap and user-fixable.
