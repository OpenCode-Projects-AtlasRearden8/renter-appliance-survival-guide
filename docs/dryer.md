# Dryer: The Appliance That Rewards Attention

Of the big five, the dryer is the one with a genuine safety story. A clogged dryer vent is a real, documented fire cause — so this chapter is less "save money" and more "sleep well."

## What error codes honestly mean

| Code family | Usually means | Often actually is |
|---|---|---|
| Airflow/vent codes (`E33`-style on some Br, `LE` on some LG, `AF` on Whirlpool-style) | Restricted airflow detected | Lint trap screen clogged with fabric softener film, crushed flex vent hose behind the machine, or a full external vent flap |
| Moisture-sensor codes / long dry times | Clothes still damp at cycle end | Sensor bars inside the drum coated with softener film — wipe them; or overshooting with a giant load; or a dying heating element (pro) |
| Heating fault (`E5/HE/F70`-family) | Heat didn't come on | Tripped thermal fuse (it dies *because* of a clogged vent) — cheap part, but **find and fix the airflow cause or it dies again, and this is the fire-risk part** |
| Door/lint (`dE/dL`) | Door circuit broken | Unlatched door, tired latch, or lint buildup in the door switch |

**The honest rule for dryers:** any code about heat or airflow is best treated as "your vent path is probably dirty somewhere between the lint screen and the outdoors." Even if cleaning doesn't clear the code, it makes the repair safer and may be the whole fix.

**Also honest:** a very common "broken dryer" is a tripped **thermal fuse** or a flipped **breaker** (electric dryers use two breakers; if one trips, the dryer spins but won't heat). Check the panel before paying anyone.

## What's worth cleaning monthly

1. **The lint screen — after every single load.** Not monthly: *every load*. And monthly, wash the screen itself with warm soapy water and a brush: dryer-sheet softener builds an invisible film that blocks airflow even when the screen looks clean. Water should flow through it freely. *FREE, 2 minutes.*
2. **The vent hose behind the machine — monthly-ish.** Pull the dryer out (it helps to have a helper; unplug electric models first, shut off the valve for gas), disconnect the flex hose, pull out the lint wad. If you see a crushed or foil-thin hose, replace it with a **semi-rigid metal duct** — foil flex hoses are a fire-risk upgrade-away. *FREE to CHEAP.*
3. **The external vent flap.** Walk outside: does the flap open freely during a cycle? Bird nests, lint mats, and paint seals kill these. *FREE.*
4. **The drum seal and moisture sensor bars.** Wipe the sensor bars (two shiny strips inside the drum, usually near the front) with rubbing alcohol monthly.
5. **Under and behind the dryer.** Lint bales collect there. Vacuum it monthly. Seriously.

**Gas dryer note:** if you have a gas dryer, the gas connection is pro territory. You can clean everything lint-related; you never take apart the gas side.

## Reset basics on a dryer

- **Full reset:** unplug for 60 seconds (electric), or breaker off for gas/integrated setups. Dryer boards are simpler than washer ones, but resets still clear sensor errors.
- **Thermal fuse logic (important):** a tripped thermal fuse is not a flaky part — it trips for a *reason*, almost always restricted airflow. If a cheap fuse fixed it and it trips again within weeks, the real fix is the vent path, and you should treat the next trip as urgent.
- **Control lock:** hold the lock-icon button 3 seconds, same as the other appliances.
- **Firmware:** Wi-Fi dryers occasionally get updates that change cycle behavior; a full reset after an update fixes ghost behaviors more often than not.

## When to call a pro — dryer edition

**STOP / call immediately if:**
- Any burning smell beyond a one-off new-belt smell (a fresh rubber/belt smell for a day or two after a belt change is normal; burning plastic is not).
- The outside of the machine or the laundry area feels unusually hot, lint is coming out of the machine seams during a cycle, or you've never cleaned the vent in years and don't plan to.
- Gas smell anywhere near a gas dryer: leave, call the gas utility's emergency line. No deliberation.

**Call a pro when:**
- A heating code returns after thermostat/fuse-level basics, or heating parts need testing with a meter (pro or confident-DIY only on an *unplugged* machine).
* The drum turns but heating doesn't, and the breaker checks out — element, thermostat, and fuse chain testing is next.
- Igniter or gas-valve questions on gas dryers — the gas side stays pro.
- The vent run is long, goes through a wall or up through the roof — professional vent cleaning tools exist and are cheap relative to a fire.

## Replace vs. keep — dryer math

- Dryers last **~10–13 years**; the repair ladder is usually lint → fuse/thermostat → element/roller/belt.
- **Replace** if: multiple heating repairs in one year, drum bearing noises, or a cracked drum.
- **Replace sooner** if it's gas-with-old-tech, or if you use it constantly and it's pre-2015: modern sensor-dry cycles genuinely save electricity versus timed-dry habits.
- Energy-hog note: a dryer with a **fully clear vent path** uses dramatically less energy than the same dryer with a dirty one. Cleaning is the efficiency upgrade before replacement is.
