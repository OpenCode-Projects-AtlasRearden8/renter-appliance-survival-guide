# Replace vs. Keep: The Energy-Hog Math in Plain Dollars

The appliance aisle will tell you everything is urgent. The truth: only two of your five appliances commonly pay for themselves in energy savings, and the decision is almost always "when it breaks," not "when a salesperson breathes." Here's the honest math.

## The two honest questions

1. **Repair vs. replace on the repair bill:** keep if the repair is under about **half the cost of a comparable new unit** — and the machine is otherwise in its years. A $180 pump in a 6-year-old washer: keep. A $600 board in a 14-year-old fridge: replace.
2. **Energy math:** only a broken-or-ancient appliance saves real money by being replaced. Ask: *how old is it, and is it running 24/7?*

## The cheat sheet

| Appliance | Lifespan | Energy math case to replace | Real replace-signals | Typical new cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Refrigerator** | 10–15 yrs | **Strong.** Pre-2010 units can cost $50–150+/yr extra, 24/7. The classic upgrade-makes-sense appliance. | Over ~12 yrs + any non-trivial repair; compressor or cracked liner | $900–2,500 |
| **Washer** | 10–14 yrs | Weak–moderate. Savings are real but slow (mostly hot water use). | Roaring drum bearing; dead control board; leaks into the motor | $550–1,200 |
| **Dryer** | 10–13 yrs | **Moderate — with a catch:** a clean vent is the big efficiency lever, not the model. Replace a working dryer only for sensor/efficiency habits. | Two heating repairs in a year; drum damage; "runs, never heats" after basics | $500–1,100 |
| **Dishwasher** | 9–10 yrs | **Weak.** Dishwashers mostly use hot water; old ones aren't dramatic hogs. Don't buy one "for efficiency." | Cracked tub/door; leak at the seals; multiple pump/electronics failures | $450–900 |
| **HVAC (AC/furnace)** | 12–20 yrs | **Strong, but bide your time.** An old AC can dominate a summer bill — but replacement is a when-it-dies or when-repairs-stack decision for most budgets. | Stacked repairs (compressor, exchanger); ancient SEER; cracked heat exchanger (non-negotiable) | $4,000–12,000+ |

## What actually blows up the bill (ranked by how much it's actually you)

1. **Old fridge** — runs constantly, decades of efficiency drift. The legit "treat yourself" upgrade.
2. **The garage/basement "extra" fridge** — silent money pit; often $100+/yr for a case of soda. Unplug the fridge of regret.
3. **Clogged anything** — dirty coils, dirty filter, lint-choked vent: all make good machines act energy-hungry. Clean before you replace.
4. **Ancient HVAC** — big monthly impact; plan for end-of-life over a couple of years, not in a panic week.
5. **Old washer** — real but modest; upgrade at failure, not before.
6. **Old dishwasher** — nearly a wash (pun intended); keep it alive, it's cheap to fix.

## The flowchart, in words

> Something broke → Cheap part + repair under ~half the cost of new + machine under ~10-12 yrs? **Fix it.** → Otherwise: is it the fridge, HVAC, or a second garage fridge? **Start the replacement math now.** → Is it a washer/dryer/dishwasher? **Fix it again, once.** More stacking failures? Replace.

## Renter-specific reality check

If you rent: the *replace* decision usually isn't yours — but the *clean* decisions are, and documented care (log those coil cleanings) is what gets a landlord to send a tech fast or a great appliance sooner. Use the [error-code log sheet](error-code-log-sheet.html) with dates through the tenancy; it's leverage that works politely.
